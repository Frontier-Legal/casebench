The demo runs a small suite end to end on your machine: six legal questions, a demo agent's answers, a folder of court decisions and a judge whose replies are recorded with it. It needs no key and calls no model: the only requests it makes are for two public model price lists, which send nothing of yours.

The cases, decisions and answers are written for the demo, about a fictional jurisdiction called Examplia. None is a real decision or legal advice.

## Run it

```sh
uvx casebench demo
```

It copies the demo project into a new temporary folder, runs the suite, prints the report and opens the workbench. `--dir` keeps the project in a folder you name; give it an absolute path, because a relative one ends up one level deeper than you asked. `--no-serve` stops after the report.

## Read the report

- **One table per task and metric.** `qa · citation_integrity` scores each answer by the share of its citations that are verified. `qa · criteria` scores the lawyer's points each answer makes. There is no single figure for the whole agent.
- **Every score has its interval.** With six cases, 71% could be anything from 29% to 92%. Six cases prove little, and the report says so.
- **All passed** is the share of answers that made every point: 50%. Half the answers missed something a lawyer said they must say.
- **Citations** counts every citation by verdict. Two of six cite a real decision for something it does not say.
- **Error rates** say how often an answer had each error, with their intervals.
- **Spend** is what the run cost, per model. The demo's judge replies are recorded, so it is $0.00.
- The **authority funnel** needs captured retrieval steps. The demo agent answers from a file, so there are none.

## Look at the failures

The workbench opens in your browser. Type your name and choose a view: "I review legal answers" for the lawyer's view, "I build or run the AI system" for the engineer's.

Open a failed answer. The evidence is on the left, the answer on the right, and each citation opens the passage it was matched to. Give a verdict on a point the judge got wrong, and the report counts yours instead. [Your first review](/lawyers/tutorials/first-review/) walks through this screen in detail.
