When your team changes the AI, a new prompt, a new model, a better search, the question is whether the new version gives better legal answers. Scores help, but a lawyer's judgment is the evidence the grading model cannot give. **Compare runs** collects it without bias: you never know which version wrote which answer until you have chosen.

## Choose the two runs

Open **Compare runs**. Pick the **later run** (the new version) and the **earlier run**. Both must have answered the same suite.

## Compare each pair

<Steps>

1. Read the question and the answer key, then **Answer A** and **Answer B**. Which version is A changes from pair to pair, and nothing on screen names a run, a score or the judge's verdict.

	<Screenshot
		name="compare-pair"
		alt="A pair to compare: the question about unused leave, Answer A and Answer B side by side, and the answer key below."
		caption="Two answers to the same question. Which run wrote which is hidden."
	/>

2. Choose **A is better** (<kbd>A</kbd>), **B is better** (<kbd>B</kbd>) or **Equal** (<kbd>E</kbd>).

3. Say why. The reason is required, and kept with your name beside the later run.

	<Screenshot
		name="compare-choice"
		alt="Answer A chosen as better, with the reason written below."
		caption="A choice and its reason."
	/>

4. Choose **Give preference and next**, or press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Enter</kbd>.

</Steps>

## Read the result

When pairs have been compared, the summary at the top counts, per practice area, the later run's wins, losses and ties, and whether the difference is significant.

<Screenshot
	name="compare-summary"
	alt="The summary: employment, 4 wins, 0 losses, 3 ties, difference +0.57 with an interval from +0.14 to +0.86, marked Not significant."
	caption="Four wins and no losses over seven pairs, and still not significant."
/>

Why is 4 wins and 0 losses "not significant"? Because seven pairs are few. Casebench calls a difference significant only when its range leaves out zero **and** a standard test of wins against losses agrees. A split as lopsided as four to nothing, either way, happens by chance about one time in eight. Compare more questions before telling anyone the new version is better.
