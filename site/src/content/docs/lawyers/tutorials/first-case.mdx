A case is a question, the answer you would give, and the points a correct answer must make. Every time your team tests the AI, it is asked your question and graded on your points. In this tutorial you write one.

You need the editor role. A reviewer can try questions and review answers, but not change the answer key.

## Open Golden data

Choose **Golden data** in the sidebar. It lists every case in each suite, with where its answer key came from and whether it is approved.

## Write the case

<Steps>

1. Choose **New case**. A form opens on the right.

2. Pick the task. **Legal question** is the usual one: the AI answers a question. Research, drafting, editing and extraction tasks have fields of their own.

3. Fill in the fields:

	| Field | What to write |
	| --- | --- |
	| Question | The question, as the AI's users put it: for a research assistant, as a lawyer would |
	| Facts | The facts the answer may rely on, if the question needs them |
	| Reference answer | The answer you would give. The grading model reads it as your evidence. |
	| Key points | One per line, each starting with its weight, such as `+3 Says the reduction needs written consent.` |
	| Statutes, Precedents | One per line, as you would cite them |
	| Practice area | Optional, such as `employment`. Reports give each figure by practice area. |

	<Screenshot
		name="golden-new"
		alt="The New case form filled in: a question about reducing salary, a reference answer, three weighted key points and the practice area employment."
		caption="Three key points: two that a correct answer earns, one that costs it."
		frame="plain"
		width={520}
	/>

4. Write the key points with care. They are what the AI is graded on.
	- A positive weight is earned when the answer makes the point. Give the decisive point the largest weight.
	- A negative weight is lost when the answer does something wrong, such as `-3 Says the employer may reduce pay unilaterally.`
	- A line without a weight weighs 1.

5. Choose **Save the case**.

</Steps>

## Who approves it

Your case joins the suite as approved, with you as its approver, unless your team requires a second editor. In that case it waits as a draft in other editors' queues until one of them approves it, and nobody approves their own change. Either way, the change log keeps your name, the time and your note.

The next time your team runs the suite, your question is asked and graded on your points.
